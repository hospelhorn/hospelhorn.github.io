Selected builds

Strategy is only useful

My foundation is product marketing: understand the market, make the strategic choice, define the position, create belief, then make it usable by buyers, sellers, executives and teams. The systems come after the judgment.

Positioning is everything

Before you automate the work, decide what you’re actually trying to change.

I’ve spent my career finding the opportunity inside complicated markets: spotting the shift, naming the problem, creating categories, sharpening the position, and turning technical capability into something buyers understand, remember and act on.

01

Category + Narrative Architecture

Change the way the market sees the problem, and you change the way it evaluates the answer.

POSITIONING

The problem

Great products lose when the market cannot quickly understand why they matter, what makes them different, or why the old way is no longer enough.

What I build

Category strategy, positioning, messaging architecture and narratives that turn technical capability into buyer value, then give the entire GTM organization one sharp story to tell.

What changes

The company competes on the criteria it wants to own. Buyers see the problem differently. Product, campaigns and sales reinforce the same market point of view.

Market shift → problem → category → position → narrative → buyer belief
Proof / pattern
Narrative architectureMARKET SHIFT → NEW PROBLEM → WHY NOW → CATEGORY → DIFFERENTIATED POSITION → BUYER VALUE

One story becomes the spine for product, campaigns, sales and market influence.

02

Agentic Sales Playbook

What if your best product marketer, sales strategist and enablement lead were in every deal?

STRATEGY

The problem

Sales knowledge is usually scattered across decks, enablement docs, tribal knowledge and methodology. It gets hard to use at the exact moment a seller needs it.

What I build

A living GTM brain connecting ICP, positioning, differentiation, buying triggers, MEDDPICC, discovery, value drivers and objection handling. A connected playbook that brings together process, methodology and narrative into one usable system.

What changes

Strategy becomes operational. Sellers can move from trigger to discovery to value to qualification with the same narrative and decision logic behind every step.

ICP + positioning → buying trigger → discovery → value → qualification → next move
Operating principle
Find the clock already running

The playbook is designed around the reason a buyer has to act, not a generic list of product capabilities.

03

Competitive Intelligence Engine

Your competitors are telling you what they're going to do next. You just have to know where to look.

MARKET

The problem

Competitive intelligence is everywhere and nowhere. Product announcements, social traction, pricing, hiring, customer proof and user sentiment move faster than static battlecards.

What I build

Always-on agents that scan public signals daily and weekly, prioritize what is commercially meaningful, and surface the few things the GTM team actually needs to know.

What changes

Teams see meaningful shifts earlier, understand why they matter, and can adapt positioning, campaigns and sales plays without drowning in monitoring work.

Public signals → detect change → rank significance → interpret → GTM action
Sample output
Signal detected · High relevance

Competitor messaging shifted from feature-led security to AI governance. Hiring and partner activity reinforce the move. Recommended action: pressure-test category language and update the next sales brief.

04

Signal Vignette

Your CRM knows more than your sellers do. I built something to fix that.

SIGNALS

The problem

Account activity is easy to collect and hard to interpret. Sellers see clicks, visits and campaign touches, but not necessarily the pattern or the reason it matters.

What I build

A weekly analyst that reviews CRM and campaign activity, finds the accounts with the strongest meaningful patterns, and turns those signals into concise account vignettes.

What changes

Sales gets a short list of accounts worth attention, with context they can actually use, instead of another dashboard to inspect.

CRM + campaign activity → pattern detection → account significance → vignette → seller action
Output
Signal, not activity

The unit of value is not another engagement metric. It is a concise explanation of what is happening at the account and why a seller should care.

05

Oscar the Outreach Officer

Why this person? Why now? Why us? Answer those three things before you write the email.

SELL

The problem

Most personalized outbound is either generic role-based copy or a research dump. Neither gives a seller a compelling reason to contact a specific buyer now.

What I build

An agent that combines verified professional context, CRM engagement, account signals and GTM positioning, identifies the strongest buying trigger and maps it to one relevant value driver and differentiator.

What changes

Research becomes reasoning. Sellers get concise outreach grounded in actual context, with a defensible answer to why this person, why now and why us.

Person + account → CRM + public signals → trigger → buyer altitude → value → outreach
Sample reasoning
Why nowREPEATED ENGAGEMENT + AI INITIATIVE → SHIP TRIGGER → EXECUTIVE ALTITUDE → AI SECURITY & GOVERNANCE

The output is the email. The value is the reasoning that makes the email worth sending.

06

Field Marketing Command Center

I got tired of running global field marketing out of spreadsheets, Slack and institutional memory. So I built the system I wanted.

DEMAND

The problem

Global field marketing creates a lot of motion: events, budgets, deadlines, leads, owners and follow-up. When those pieces live in separate systems, execution gets harder to see and harder to govern.

What I build

A single command center for large conferences through 1:1 field activity, with gated checklists, budgets, lead processes, status, ownership and reporting connected in one operating view.

What changes

Teams know what is happening, what is at risk, what is late, what it costs and what needs attention without chasing updates across spreadsheets and Slack.

Plan → gate → budget → execute → capture → follow up → report
Design choice
Make the process visible

The system turns field activity into an operating portfolio, with risk and accountability surfaced before the event happens.

07

Pipeline Intelligence

A pipeline dashboard should tell you where to act, not just where you missed.

MEASURE

The problem

Pipeline reporting can become a rear-view mirror: lots of numbers, little clarity on where performance is changing or what deserves attention next.

What I build

An executive review that connects attainment, channel trends, stage movement, regional performance and risk into one view of what's working, what's at risk and where to act.

What changes

The conversation moves from reporting activity to identifying risk, finding leverage and deciding where to act.

Pipeline data → trends → risk → channel + stage diagnosis → action
Executive view
Attention required

Attainment is pacing behind plan. The issue is concentrated in one channel and two stages, not broad-based. Focus: increase creation in-channel and inspect stage conversion before adding spend.

08

Campaign + Launch Engine

The strategy shouldn't disappear somewhere between the product brief and the campaign.

CREATE

The problem

Launches often start with technical source material and then fragment into disconnected briefs, assets and channel requests. The story gets diluted as execution expands.

What I build

A system that starts with product truth and positioning, then translates it by audience, vertical and desired outcome into coordinated launch narratives, campaign sequences and channel-ready work.

What changes

The strategy survives execution. Teams move faster without sacrificing narrative consistency, and customization happens from a shared point of view instead of from scratch.

Technical truth → positioning → audience + outcome → narrative → campaign → channel execution
Translation layer
One strategy, many expressions

The goal is not content generation. It is preserving the strategic idea as it moves from product to market.

09

Intelligent Lead Routing

A hot lead shouldn't die waiting for somebody to notice it.

OPERATE

The problem

A lead can be valuable and still disappear into process. Scoring, enrichment, ownership, alerts and follow-up often happen in different places and at different speeds.

What I build

A responsive workflow that scores fit, enriches context, routes ownership, triggers Slack alerts, enforces SLAs and gives the next person useful prompts for follow-up.

What changes

High-value demand moves faster, handoffs become explicit, and context travels with the lead instead of getting lost between systems.

Signal → ICP score → enrich → route → alert → SLA → follow-up
Operating principle
Context should travel with the work

Automation is most useful when it improves the handoff, not merely the routing rule.

What it changes
